What the backtest says

INTERPRETIVE
  • Helene's biggest failure was the plant, which is outside a lift-station ladder's scope.
  • The reporting unit is the manhole, so no station-level hit rate is possible from public data.
  • Milton released about four times Helene's sewage volume with zero surge. That is the strongest argument for treating grid power and rainfall inflow as first-class hazards, and against a surge-only console.

How to use the public planning console

INTERPRETIVE · operator guide
  1. Check evidence first. Monitor shows NOAA/NHC/NWS timestamps and failures. A downloaded record is not necessarily a current measurement; unavailable data do not mean safe conditions.
  2. Choose a dataset. Public stations defaults to 82 City-owned/maintained records; all 131 records include other published owners. Register status is not live pump status. Check the source Facility ID and station ID before correlating a record. Unknown county-zone matches stay unknown; planning zones do not establish an active evacuation order.
  3. Define an impact exercise. Projected impact defaults to the real City subset and a usable future NOAA OFS peak. You can instead choose a scenario level while public basin/Census and annual treatment-throughput context stay automatic. Its local gauge conversion and flat terrain plane are screening assumptions, not a flood or outage forecast. The separate fictional network supports reproducible topology exercises.
  4. Supply assumptions explicitly. Automatic population sums unique 2020 Census blocks in source basin footprints associated with screened locations; it does not measure customers, flooded residents or outages. Published annual treatment flow is separate City reference context. Optional local overrides can supply explicit duration, population/generation or repair/funding assumptions; terrain screening does not establish damage, and no reimbursement or outage duration is automatic.
  5. Review the ten dimensions. Response plan shares the exact station scope and water-level input with Projected impact; the latest usable NOAA peak is not rounded to a ladder step. Open its all-ten register, choose the public/local or operator track and review missing-input mitigations as well as scenario criteria. Real flow/topology-dependent metrics remain not assessable. Open the station review to see the actual public IDs driving the screen; descriptive metrics are not scored against synthetic thresholds. Review acknowledgments reset when evidence/scenario changes; they do not mark completed operations.
  6. Use optional public context and share the basis. Load dated environmental/GIS records on demand; failures retain an explicitly labeled last retrieval. Export assumptions/results or print the selected view for review. Only the responsible authority’s current instructions, verified local telemetry and approved SOP establish operational action.

Method

INTERPRETIVE

The console applies the ten-metric diagnostic suite from a contamination-resilient cluster-randomized-trial (CRT) design to a wastewater lift-station network. At a given scenario level the stations at or below a hypothetical flat water plane are one arm (exposed) and the rest are the other (unexposed). This is an exploratory adaptation of CRT geometric diagnostics to planning scenarios, not a validated equivalence between trial contamination bias and wastewater cascade dynamics. Actual pumped-flow propagation requires verified connections and operating telemetry, which this public inventory does not provide.

The suite has three pillars: Statistical Interchangeability (metrics 1 to 3), Topological and Spatial Cohesion (4 to 7) and Contamination Resilience (8 to 10). Each metric has a threshold; when it is crossed, the console shows the sub-optimal condition, an analytical mitigation (how to read the numbers) and an operational mitigation (what to do in the field). The response plan collects the operational mitigations of the sub-optimal metrics into timeline phases.

Live feeds set context only: the gauge reading and the OFS forecast map onto the ladder, and the NHC track suggests a phase. Nothing here predicts which station fails.

How the synthetic network was calibrated

MODELED · computed liveMEASURED · real aggregates

The network is generated in your browser from a fixed seed (mulberry32, seed 20260904), so every visitor sees the same 130 stations. Ground elevations are drawn inside bands whose counts reproduce the real network's surge ladder and storm planes exactly, with a few fixed values pinning the published quantiles. Stations sit on a fictional peninsula in a local kilometer frame (barrier islands and keys, causeways, tidal creeks, an inland ridge): lower stations on islands, shorelines and creek lowlands, higher ones inland. The fictional 15-edge graph, evacuation-zone distribution and criticality flags preserve archived calibration summaries. The predecessor inferred its 15 directional pairs from terrain, rather than verified pumped flow; that geometry is kept only as a synthetic exercise. The three treatment plants are the terminals: each force-main sink links, schematically, to its basin's plant (these links are not counted as edges). The archived calibration contains 130 elevation-linked records. The separate current public register has 131 records, including one with no FACILITYID; the newly retrieved terrain snapshot covers all 131. The fictional 130-station calibration is retained for reproducibility and is not presented as the current public register. What is withheld, and why

INTERPRETIVE

No nonpublic facility coordinates or nonpublic per-station data are published. The separate Public stations view contains agency-published asset records from official public GIS, with an ownership filter plus official county zone overlays, with their provenance and limits. Its elevations are independently retrieved USGS raster-terrain estimates with NAVD88 datum and acquisition metadata, not surveyed equipment heights. It contains no operational telemetry or verified flow connections. The synthetic scenario remains unlinked to those official asset IDs; its per-station elevations, zones, topology and criticality are fictional. Archived private-network evidence remains at network-level aggregate only: NOAA gauge readings, storm-level backtest counts, publicly reported sewage volumes, and network-level summaries (counts, shares, the ten metric values). The synthetic IDs (LS-001 to LS-130) and names are neutral and do not correspond to real facilities.

Data sources

MEASURED · public feeds
  • NOAA CO-OPS station 8726520, St. Petersburg, Tampa Bay: observed water level (6-minute), tide predictions, and the Operational Forecast System (OFS) water-level guidance, via the CO-OPS Data API.
  • NOAA National Hurricane Center forecast and observed positions, via the Esri Living Atlas Active Hurricanes feature service.
  • National Weather Service API (api.weather.gov), active alerts at the gauge point.
  • Archived calibration summaries (2026-09-04): terrain, scenario zone split and inferred geometric links, retained at network level. These are distinct from the verified 2026-10-23 public register/zone overlay and USGS terrain provenance.
  • NOAA 8726520 datums: local NAVD88−MLLW = 1.48 ft, epoch 1983–2001, accepted 6 Sep 2022. This offset does not establish a uniform bay-wide water surface or convert an NHC above-ground inundation product.
  • Backtest volumes: FDEP Public Notice of Pollution, City of St. Petersburg disclosures, and press reports quoting the City.

All feeds are keyless and fetched by your browser. Nothing is stored or sent anywhere else.

Caveats

INTERPRETIVE
  • Ground elevation is not wet-well or panel elevation. The real calibration uses USGS 3DEP ground elevation, so the ladder tiers are screening flags, not flood lines.
  • The archived topology inferred direction from terrain. Pumped force-main flow does not necessarily follow downhill ground. It is retained as fictional exercise geometry; the real public register has no verified connections.
  • The gauge is one point. Surge varies across a bay; a single gauge reading stands in for the whole network.
  • Surge is one hazard. Grid power and rainfall inflow drove Milton's releases; this console does not model them.
  • Feeds can lag or drop. Every panel shows its feed state and timestamp; a stale or unavailable feed is labeled as such.

Utility hook

MODELED · empty by default

The script defines STATUS_OVERRIDE = {}. A utility could wire it to SCADA: keys are station IDs and values look like {status: 'down' | 'surcharged' | 'ok', note: '...'}. An overridden station is drawn with a cross on the map and its status appears in the station panel. In this public copy it is empty, so nothing is overridden.
