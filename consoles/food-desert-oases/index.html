See how the USDA Food Access Research Atlas classifies a census tract, and find the nearest SNAP-authorized stores, SNAP-authorized farmers markets and listed food banks and meal programs.

Privacy: this page has no server of its own and saves nothing. Your location is not stored, not put in the page address and not kept after you leave. To search, your browser sends the text you type to OpenStreetMap Nominatim, and the searched point's coordinates to the USDA services (gisportal.ers.usda.gov and the SNAP retailer layer hosted on ArcGIS Online, services1.arcgis.com) and to the OpenStreetMap Overpass server listed under Sources (plus the maps.mail.ru Overpass mirror, only if the main server does not answer). Map tiles for the area you view load from tile.openstreetmap.org. Use my location skips the text search.

Area status: USDA Food Access Research Atlas

Search for a place to see how the Atlas classifies its census tract.

Data vintage: 2025 SNAP-authorized Retailer Access Map, released July 2026; store locations as of June 2025; 2020 census tracts and population; 2020-2024 American Community Survey (ACS) income.
What it measures: straight-line distance to the nearest SNAP-authorized store of any kind, including convenience stores. It is not the older supermarket-based measure. It describes the whole census tract, not any one household. Distances are straight-line, not travel distance. The Atlas covers the 50 states and DC only.

Older measure: 2019 Atlas (supermarkets and large grocery stores, 2010 census tracts)

    What this does not cover

    • No complete national food pantry list. No complete, open national list of food pantries was found that this page can use. Pantries and meal programs appear here only where volunteers have tagged them in OpenStreetMap, so many are missing. For a full local list, dial 2-1-1 (or visit 211.org) or contact your regional food bank, which you can find with the Feeding America food bank locator.
    • SNAP authorization only. The SNAP retailer list shows that a store is authorized to accept SNAP. It does not show hours, stock or prices.
    • Farmers markets that take SNAP only. The full USDA farmers market directory (USDA AMS Local Food Portal) is not available to this page without an access key, so markets that do not accept SNAP are missing.
    • Not listed: WIC vendors, school meals and senior meal programs.
    • Territories. The Food Access Research Atlas covers the 50 states and DC only. Puerto Rico, American Samoa and the Northern Mariana Islands use separate nutrition assistance programs instead of SNAP, so few or no SNAP stores are listed there.
    • Straight-line distances. All distances are straight-line ("as the crow flies"), not travel distance by road or transit.
    • Tracts, not households. Atlas flags describe a whole census tract, not any one household or block.

    Sources

    Every list shows its own data date from the source. Nothing on this page is cached from elsewhere or filled in when a source fails.

    Place search: OpenStreetMap Nominatim

    nominatim.openstreetmap.org, used only when you press Search, at most one request per second (usage policy).

    Data © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL 1.0. Date: live OpenStreetMap data, no release date.

    USDA ERS Food Access Research Atlas, 2025

    Food Access Research Atlas, SNAP-authorized Retailer Access Map, straight-line distance (map service FARA_2025_StraightLine, layer 4).

    Date: 2025 edition, released July 2026; stores as of June 2025; 2020 census tracts and population; 2020-2024 ACS income. Citation: U.S. Department of Agriculture, Economic Research Service. Food Access Research Atlas, SNAP-authorized Retailer Access Map.

    USDA ERS Food Access Research Atlas, 2019 (older measure)

    Food Access Research Atlas, 2019 edition (map service FARA_2019, layer 30).

    Date: 2019 edition; distance to supermarkets and large grocery stores; 2010 census tracts. Not comparable with the 2025 measure.

    SNAP-authorized retailers: USDA Food and Nutrition Administration

    SNAP Retailer Locator data (public ArcGIS dataset), excluding farmers markets.

    Date: shown after a search, from the layer's last edit date. The source says it updates every 2 weeks.

    SNAP-authorized farmers markets: USDA Food and Nutrition Administration

    The same SNAP retailer data, store type "Farmers and Markets". Only markets and farmers authorized to accept SNAP are included.

    Date: shown after a search, from the layer's last edit date.

    Food banks and meal programs: OpenStreetMap via Overpass

    overpass-api.de, with the maps.mail.ru mirror as a fallback. Places tagged social_facility=food_bank, amenity=food_bank or social_facility=soup_kitchen within 50 km (31 miles).

    © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL. Date: shown after a search, from the OSM data timestamp, plus each place's last edit date.

    About. A volunteer, unofficial tool that reads public USDA and OpenStreetMap data for the place you search. Listings can be missing, out of date or briefly unavailable. It is not an eligibility decision or a complete directory. Confirm hours, stock, eligibility and SNAP acceptance with each place, and dial 2-1-1 for local food help.
