How to read "strain" honestly. The headline number is each region's demand as a % of its own highest reported demand in the snapshot's 7-day window — a pressure proxy, not a reserve margin. A region can sit at 100% of its recent peak and still be fine if it has spare capacity; this dashboard can't see true capacity or reserves (that's the grid operators' real-time data). Treat high numbers as "working hard / worth watching," not "about to fail." The interchange arrows are real, directional public data (region-to-region net power flow), but grid physics is loop-governed (Kirchhoff), so flows distribute across all paths — this is the net result, not a simple upstream→downstream chain.
Data: EIA Hourly Electric Grid Monitor (EIA-930), raw reported values from the official balance CSV and interchange CSV, downloaded 23 October 2026. No imputed or adjusted demand is substituted. Reports can lag, have gaps and be revised; each BA has its own timestamp. Published reports are checked automatically; the embedded snapshot remains available if an update cannot be loaded. Fuel bars show positive generation only; storage charging and other negative entries are omitted. Optional personal-key updates use the EIA OpenData v2 API.
